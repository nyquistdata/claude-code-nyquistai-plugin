# PMA Strategy Report Template

Use this structure for the Markdown report. Keep every section; if one doesn't apply, say so in a sentence and why (e.g. "Not a combination product: no drug or biologic component"). Sections 3, 5 and 7 usually decide success, so give them the most depth.

```markdown
# PMA Submission Strategy: [Device Name]

**Prepared:** [date] · **FDA data pulled:** [date] via NyquistAI connector · **Status:** Draft for internal review

---

## Executive Summary
Three short paragraphs: (1) the device and its intended use; (2) the recommended pathway and PMA type, the core evidence package, and the main precedent; (3) the top 3 risks, the estimated timeline to approval, and the immediate next steps.

## 1. Device Description and Intended Use
- Device description: principle of operation, components, materials, energy, software, patient contact
- Proposed Indications for Use (verbatim, as confirmed with the user)
- Target population, clinical setting, user
- Development stage and existing data

## 2. Regulatory Pathway Rationale
- Classification: product code, 21 CFR regulation (or "not classified, post-amendments"), device class, review panel / medical specialty
- Why PMA: risk profile, absence of a valid predicate, regulation text. Include any evidence pointing to De Novo or 510(k) and why it was or wasn't accepted
- PMA type recommendation (traditional, modular, PDP) with rationale. Note HDE if the population is small enough to qualify
- Breakthrough Device designation: eligibility assessment against the statutory criteria
- Combination product considerations (primary mode of action, lead center, need for an RFD)

## 3. Regulatory Landscape and Precedent
Precedent table:

| PMA # | Device | Applicant | Product Code | Decision Date | Pivotal Study | Panel? | PAS Required? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Then a short analysis per key precedent (from its SSED): indications, pivotal design, endpoints and performance goals, sample size, follow-up, and what this means for the new device. Close with relevant FDA guidance (title, status, date, link) and consensus standards.

## 4. FDA Interaction Plan
Pre-Submission plan with a table of proposed questions:

| # | Topic | Proposed Question for FDA | Why We're Asking | Timing |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Include IDE-related Pre-Subs, Breakthrough interactions if applicable, and the 100-day meeting after filing.

## 5. Nonclinical Testing Strategy
Table by category: bench performance, biocompatibility (ISO 10993 endpoints by contact type and duration), sterilization and shelf life, packaging, electrical safety and EMC, software, cybersecurity, MRI safety, human factors, GLP animal studies. For each: test, standard or guidance, rationale (precedent SSED or guidance), and status.

## 6. Clinical Strategy
- IDE: significant-risk determination, early feasibility / feasibility / pivotal sequence
- Proposed pivotal design: comparator or performance goal, primary safety and effectiveness endpoints, key secondary endpoints, follow-up duration
- Sample size basis: assumptions, with precedent trial sizes for reference
- Use of OUS or existing data (21 CFR 812.28 / 814.15)
- Comparable trials from the clinical landscape search (table)
- Site strategy and BIMO readiness

## 7. Manufacturing and Quality System
QMSR readiness, manufacturing section content, critical suppliers, preapproval inspection plan, and common inspection findings in this device area.

## 8. Benefit-Risk Assessment
Planned framing per FDA's PMA benefit-risk guidance: magnitude and probability of benefit, nature of risks, uncertainty, patient perspective, available alternatives. Use known post-market problems for this product code (MAUDE, recalls) to anticipate risk questions.

## 9. Labeling Strategy
IFU structure, key warnings and contraindications expected from precedent labeling and post-market data, patient labeling needs, and the evidence each claim depends on.

## 11. Advisory Panel Preparedness
Likelihood of a panel (novelty, precedent panel history, safety signals), likely panel questions, and preparation plan.

## 12. Post-Approval Obligations
Expected post-approval study conditions (from precedent approval orders), annual reports, MDR, and a plan for future supplements (180-day, panel-track, real-time, 30-day notice).

## 13. Timeline, Review Goals and Budget
Critical-path timeline from today to approval (table or phase list), current MDUFA review goals (cited), PMA user fee and small-business eligibility (cited), and major cost drivers.

## 14. Risk Register

| # | Risk | Area | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Aim for 8–12 real risks across regulatory, clinical, technical, manufacturing and commercial.

## 15. Recommended Next Steps
Numbered list of the next 5–8 actions with suggested timing.

## Appendices
A. Search log (tool, query, filters, date, hits). B. Full precedent list. C. Guidance and standards list with links. D. Assumptions and open questions.

---
*This report supports, and does not replace, review by qualified regulatory professionals and FDA feedback through the Pre-Submission program.*
```
